STILL Becoming

What I reined for

In darkness emerges a ray of hope,
I start with a trot and then gallop,
Following the light, I reach the hilltop,
Beneath twinkling lights, I cherish
every dewdrop.

I am the rider I longed to see,
It’s no longer just a wish in me,
The dream I held is come to be.

PLAYING FOR NOBODY BUT ME

The clock on the backstage wall ticked past three in the morning, its pale face barely visible in the dim hall. Outside, the city was buried under frost and silence, the kind that swallows sound before it even reaches the window. But inside the empty theater, Maya stood at the edge of the dark stage. Her fingers were cold, stiff from hours of waiting around for a moment like this one. She hadn’t come here to sleep. She’d come to take the one thing the day never gave her — time nobody else was using. She crossed to the switchboard and threw a single lever. High in the rafters, a tungsten bulb buzzed to life, dropping a hard, narrow cone of light straight onto the grand piano. Everything outside that circle disappeared at once — the empty rows of red velvet seats, the balconies, the shadows crowding the wings. All of it gone, swallowed whole. The spotlight didn’t just light the piano. It cut the room in half, and left her alone with the only part of it that mattered. She stepped into the light and sat down at the bench. When her hands finally touched the ivory, the exhaustion that had sat on her shoulders all day let go, all at once, like something had finally been given permission to leave. In its place came something else — restless, hungry, wide awake. She didn’t need anyone in those seats to make this worth doing. The silence of an empty theater at three in the morning was the only kind of listening she’d ever fully trusted. Her hands found the first chord before she’d even decided to play it. The sound filled the dark room and came back to her off the balconies, off the velvet, off walls that hadn’t heard music in hours — and for the first time all day, nobody was waiting for her to be anything other than exactly this. She played without thinking about what came next, just letting her hands move where they wanted. A scale that bent into something else. A chord held too long, then let go. Nobody was grading this.
